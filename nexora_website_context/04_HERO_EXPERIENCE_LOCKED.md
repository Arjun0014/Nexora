# Hero Experience — Locked Creative Direction

This is the most developed part of the project.

Do not redesign the hero concept from scratch unless explicitly asked.

---

# Core concept

## One World, Many Workforces

The hero is a cinematic circular architectural diorama representing five Nexora service worlds.

The experience begins as a complete miniature world, then scroll takes the user into individual sectors.

The key creative device is:

> **motion → time freeze → movement between worlds → time freeze**

---

# 1. Opening master world

A premium circular 3D architectural diorama is centered in the viewport.

### Geometry

- one circular platform
- exactly five wedge-shaped sectors
- thick dark bronze / black physical base
- open top
- open outer front of each wedge
- tall radial divider walls between sectors
- small landscaped central hub
- miniature architectural scale

### Why the divider walls are tall

They are not decorative.

When the camera enters a sector at near-horizontal eye level:
- the left divider wall hides the neighboring sector
- the right divider wall hides the neighboring sector
- only the current world is visible

Each sector works like an open-front theatre stage.

### Master camera

High 3/4 top-down overview.

The full circle is visible and obviously 3D.

---

# 2. Opening idle loop

The master world has an accepted looping animation.

### Locked behavior

- duration: 15 seconds
- exactly one 360° clockwise rotation
- fixed camera
- model rotates as one rigid solid object
- all people remain frozen
- all architecture remains fixed to the disc
- no zoom
- no orbiting camera
- no morphing
- no phasing
- no geometry drift
- seamless return to start

The loop exists to make the opening feel alive even before the user scrolls.

# 3. The five sector worlds

## A. Hospitality

**Palette**
- warm amber
- champagne gold
- cream
- dark wood

**Close-view composition**
- camera outside the open wedge looking inward
- near-horizontal
- left 35–40% relatively clean for website copy
- main action on right

**Frozen action**
A professional waiter is caught mid-pour.

Visible evidence of frozen time:
- liquid stream suspended in mid-air
- guest hand partially reaching
- subtle fabric / napkin motion may also be frozen

**Feeling**
Luxury service, warmth, guest experience.

---

## B. Events & Promotions

**Palette**
- violet
- magenta
- deep plum
- polished silver
- restrained electric accents

**Frozen action**
Event hostess / registration professional is caught during badge handover.

Visible evidence of frozen time:
- lanyard suspended and curved between hands
- attendee reaching but not yet holding it
- background attendee caught mid-step

**Feeling**
Premium GCC corporate event, social energy, professional execution.

Avoid nightclub styling.

---

## C. Facilities & Support

**Palette**
- soft teal
- warm white
- pale stone
- muted green

**Frozen action**
Housekeeping / facility professional is caught unfolding a clean towel or linen.

Visible evidence:
- fabric suspended in the air mid-unfold
- body interrupted mid-action
- elegant service trolley nearby

**Feeling**
Immaculate, calm, precise, dependable.

Do not make it look like a hospital.

---

## D. Specialist / Technical Workforce

**Palette**
- steel blue
- graphite
- restrained orange
- metallic industrial surfaces

**Frozen action**
Technician is caught adjusting an exposed technical connection / machine.

Visible evidence:
- hand interrupted mid-action
- flexible cable or component suspended mid-swing
- frozen maintenance sparks / particles may be used to make the freeze unmistakable

**Feeling**
Precision, technical competence, specialist operations.

This must not look like construction.

---

## E. Recruitment & Workforce Management

**Palette**
- deep navy
- slate blue
- cyan accents
- soft white

**Frozen action**
Recruiter / workforce coordinator is caught transferring an onboarding document to a candidate.

Visible evidence:
- paper slightly airborne
- candidate hand reaching but not touching
- optional loose page / folder corner mid-turn

**Feeling**
Strategic, organized, human, professional.

Do not make it feel like a generic call center.

# 4. Close sector camera rule

For every sector:

- camera sits OUTSIDE the circular disc
- camera looks INWARD through the open front of the wedge
- near-horizontal eye level
- two radial divider walls frame the scene
- neighboring sectors are completely hidden
- main action is on the RIGHT
- LEFT 35–40% is calmer for text
- the central tree / hub should NOT be visibly repeated in close views

Close scenes must feel like self-contained worlds.

---

# 5. Time-freeze rule

This is essential.

The frames must never look like ordinary posed corporate photographs.

Every frozen scene needs physical evidence that time stopped during real motion.

Examples:
- suspended liquid
- suspended lanyard
- suspended linen
- suspended cable / sparks
- suspended document

No motion blur in the frozen state.

The viewer should understand:

> the world stopped, but the camera is free to move.

---

# 6. Transition language

## First transition

The overview-to-Hospitality transition has already been explored and an accepted rotating entry direction exists.

Do not redesign it casually.

## Sector-to-sector transitions

The repeated motion grammar is:

1. current frozen action briefly resumes
2. camera moves sideways toward the RIGHT radial divider wall
3. wall completely fills / occludes the viewport
4. environment changes while hidden
5. camera emerges into the next sector
6. the next sector action is briefly moving
7. TIME STOPS on the exact final freeze frame

Typical transition duration:
**approximately 5 seconds**

The divider wall acts as a physical wipe, deliberately hiding generative continuity problems.

### Critical rule

No visible:
- morphing
- phasing
- dissolving
- environment transformation
- teleporting

The sector change happens only while the wall fully hides the view.

---

# 7. Text overlay behavior

The close compositions intentionally preserve the left side for copy.

Exact copy is not locked.

Claude Design should decide:

- amount of copy
- progress indicator
- sector naming
- CTA behavior

Do not place heavy UI over the right-side action.
